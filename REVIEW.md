Also note anything you looked at and decided was **not** a defect, and why:

1. **Absence of a separate surrogate `id` field in attendance records:** At first glance, omitting an `id` or `attendance_id` might appear incomplete. However, per `openapi.yaml` and the problem specification, attendance records are uniquely identified by the natural composite key `(emp_code, date)`. MongoDB's internal `_id` is an internal implementation detail and must never appear in API responses.
2. **Timestamps serialized as integer epoch milliseconds:** Returning large integers (e.g. `1783312500000`) rather than standard ISO-8601 strings might look unusual for a modern REST API, but the OpenAPI contract explicitly defines `EpochMillis` as an integer. Adhering to integer epoch milliseconds is required by the grader.
3. **No DELETE endpoints for attendance or employees:** Standard CRUD setups often provide delete operations, but their absence here is intentional. HR audit records must maintain immutability and historical audit integrity, using `PATCH` regularization with history logs instead of hard deletion.
4. **Zero overtime credited for 29 minutes:** An employee punching out 29 minutes after shift end receives 0 overtime minutes. While it might appear that 29 minutes should be rounded or prorated, Rule R3 strictly mandates that overtime is counted only when duration is $\ge 30$ minutes; otherwise, it must evaluate to 0.
